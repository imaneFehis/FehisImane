01 · The problem

A long, regulated road to the first payment

Cresora is payments infrastructure for healthcare and other regulated industries. Software companies (ISV partners) connect Cresora to their products so their clients, such as dental clinics, physical therapy practices and urgent care centers, can take payments.

Before that first payment, a partner has to integrate and test, sign a HIPAA Business Associate Agreement, complete a PCI questionnaire, pass certification in three tiers and get each merchant approved. Cresora's own teams review every step.

So the core design question was: how do you keep people oriented in a process with this many steps, owners and ways to fail?

02 · Constraints

What shaped the design

Regulation

HIPAA and PCI

Compliance documents have real statuses and expiry dates, and every action on money or health data needs to be traceable.

Users

10+ roles

Four partner roles with different permissions, and six internal teams that each own a different review step.

Scope

Two portals, one system

Partner and admin portals share one visual language and UI kit, so a status means the same thing everywhere.

03 · Users

Two sides, many jobs

Partner side

Software companies integrating Cresora

Technical lead: integration, testing, API keys, webhooks, certification.

Operations lead: onboarding progress, merchants, go-live readiness.

Team roles: Owner, Admin, Developer and Compliance, each with its own permissions.

Cresora internal

Teams that review and approve

Admin, Risk Ops, Underwriting, Pre-underwriting, Compliance and Support. Each one reviews, approves or monitors a different part of the partner and merchant journey.

04 · Key decisions

Three calls that defined the product

Decision 01

Always show where you are and what blocks you

In a long, regulated process, partners kept needing the same answer: what's left before we can go live, and is it on us or on Cresora?

Separate pages per task

Not chosen

One page each for integration, compliance, certification and merchants. Simple to build, but partners would have to visit every page to work out what still blocks launch.

Stage-aware path

Chosen

A dashboard that adapts to each stage (first login, sandbox, certification, live), an onboarding checklist, and a go-live gate that lists exactly what blocks launch: blocked, in review, all passed, approved.

Why

Every screen tells users their current stage and what comes next, so nobody has to email support to find out where they stand.

Trade-off

Many more states to design and keep in sync. The dashboard changes across four stages, and every checklist step needs done, in progress, failed and blocked versions.

Decision 02

Add friction where money moves

Voids, refunds, reversals and chargebacks can't be undone. Speed matters for operations teams, but one wrong click costs real money.

One-click actions

Not chosen

Faster for operations teams handling many transactions, but one misclick on a refund or void moves real money and can't be undone.

Confirm every irreversible action

Chosen

A confirmation step for each irreversible payment action, with the transaction's full lifecycle visible: authorize, capture, reverse, void, partial or full refund, ACH states and chargebacks.

Why

In payments, a moment of friction is cheaper than an incorrect refund.

Trade-off

Each irreversible action takes one extra step. I kept it to a single focused dialog with a required reason, so it only slows people down where it matters.

Decision 03

Make overrides accountable

Admins sometimes need to waive a certification check or override the go-live gate. That power is necessary, and also risky.

A simple override switch

Not chosen

Quick for admins, but it leaves no record of who bypassed a check or why, which is exactly what a compliance review would flag.

Reason required, always logged

Chosen

Gate overrides and waived checks can't be saved without a written reason. Every action lands in an audit log with event details, and review work runs through queues.

Why

Payments and healthcare data demand accountability: anyone should be able to see who changed what, and why.

Trade-off

Overrides take longer, and admins must write a reason every time. On a platform handling payments and health data, that accountability is worth it.
