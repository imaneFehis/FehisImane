02 · The problem

Rides ran on phone calls

MobiRide is the platform for Mobilis's transport department, the team that manages company vehicles and drivers. Employees request rides to work sites, managers confirm missions, admins assign drivers and drivers carry out the rides.

The existing process made it hard to find available drivers, to follow a ride's status, and to communicate when something went wrong. Four groups of people depended on the same missions, and none of them had the full picture.

03 · Research

Four groups, four views

I interviewed each group about how they work today, what slows them down, and what would make their day easier.

Employees

No confirmation

Once a ride was done they got nothing back, so they weren't sure about their request.

Drivers

Unclear tasks

Task details weren't always clear, which led to confusion and delays.

Admins

Slow matching

A complicated dashboard made it hard to find available drivers quickly.

Everyone

No live status

No real-time tracking and no easy way to communicate about delays.

05 · Key decisions

Two calls worth explaining

Decision 01

One big action per step for drivers

Drivers use the app on the move, often in a hurry. Every extra tap or hidden menu became a missed update.

A full mission screen

Not chosen

All details and actions on one screen. Complete, but drivers on the move would have to hunt for the right button, which is exactly where tasks were being misread.

Task in the notification, one primary button

Chosen

The essential task details arrive in the notification itself, and each step has one large, visible action: Start ride, then Ride completed.

Why

Drivers misread tasks and missed actions. Fewer choices per screen means fewer mistakes, and every tap updates the status for everyone else.

Trade-off

Less information at once. Anything beyond the essentials sits one tap away, so the main screen stays focused on the next action.

Decision 02

Suggest drivers instead of making admins search

Admins had to hunt through a cluttered dashboard to find who was free, while missions waited.

Fully automatic assignment

Not chosen

Fastest on paper, but admins know things the system doesn't, like who knows a site or which vehicle suits a trip. Removing that judgment would cause mistakes and pushback.

Suggestions, admin decides

Chosen

Driver suggestions based on availability and location, inside request lists organized by status tabs and filters. The admin keeps the final call.

Why

It removes the search without taking away the admin's judgment about people and vehicles.

Trade-off

Admins still make the final call, so assignment isn't instant. But they no longer have to search for who is free.
